Democrats Claim Bipartisanship on Lewiston Memorial – But Their Record Says Otherwise

By Matthew Hogan · August 19, 2026

The unanimous 13-0 vote in the Joint Committee on Veterans and Legal Affairs to advance LD 2041, the Lewiston memorial bill, was a rare moment of bipartisanship in Augusta. Republicans and Democrats stood together to honor the eighteen lives lost and the thirteen others wounded in the deadliest mass shooting in Maine history.

Sen. Peggy Rotundo (D-Lewiston) called it a "clear message" that we will not forget. Sen. Richard Bradstreet (R-Kennebec) rightly called it "a duty to pass."

But let's not be naive about what's happening behind the scenes. While Democrats are happy to pose for photos and claim credit for a memorial, they are simultaneously using the tragedy to push a gun control agenda that would do nothing to prevent the next Lewiston – and would, in fact, infringe on the constitutional rights of law-abiding Mainers.

The Facts They Don't Want You to Know

The independent commission that investigated the Lewiston shooting found that law enforcement had "sufficient probable cause" to take the shooter into protective custody before the attack. Multiple missed opportunities – by the U.S. military, by local law enforcement, and by the shooter's own family – contributed to the tragedy. The shooter was known to be in crisis. He had been hospitalized for mental health issues. He had access to weapons despite clear warning signs.

None of this was a failure of gun laws. It was a failure of enforcement and communication.

Democrats will tell you otherwise. They will point to the "red flag" law passed via citizen initiative in 2025, or the "yellow flag" law passed in 2024, and claim they are making Maine safer. But those laws were already on the books before the shooting. They didn't stop Robert Card. They wouldn't have stopped him. He was already prohibited from possessing firearms under federal law, and yet he still obtained them.

The Real Agenda

The memorial bill, LD 2041, is about healing. It appropriates $500,000 from the state's general fund for a permanent memorial and a mental health resource center at the site of the former Schemengees Bar and Grille and Just-In-Time Recreation. That is a worthy cause. Republicans support it.

But Democrats aren't stopping there. Even as they vote for the memorial, they are quietly pushing for new restrictions – expanded background checks, magazine capacity limits, and bans on certain firearms – that would do nothing to prevent a tragedy like Lewiston. The shooter passed a background check. He used legally purchased firearms. The problem was not the guns; it was the system that failed to identify and intervene with a man in crisis.

Fiscal Hypocrisy

Some Republicans have raised legitimate concerns about the $500,000 appropriation amid broader budget negotiations. That's responsible governance. Meanwhile, Democrats who lecture us about the need for "common sense gun laws" have no problem spending taxpayer dollars on studies, commissions, and new bureaucratic layers that deliver nothing but reports.

If Democrats truly cared about preventing gun violence, they would focus on fixing the mental health crisis system – the one that failed Robert Card and his victims. They would demand accountability from law enforcement agencies that failed to act on clear warning signs. They would invest in treatment, not restrictions.

But that's not the political play. The play is to use the memory of the victims to advance a broader anti-gun agenda, knowing that Republicans will be accused of "politicizing tragedy" if they object.

The Bottom Line

The Lewiston memorial is not a political issue. It is a human issue. When the full Legislature votes on August 26, Republicans should vote yes. They should honor the victims and support their families.

But they should also call out Democrats for the hypocrisy. You can't claim bipartisanship on the memorial while simultaneously using the tragedy to strip away the rights of law-abiding Mainers. The families of the victims deserve a place to heal. They do not deserve to have their loved ones' memory weaponized for political gain.

This memorial must be a marker of remembrance – not a monument to an anti-gun agenda. Mainers deserve better than politicians who turn tragedy into talking points. Let's pass the memorial, honor the fallen, and then get to work on the real reforms that actually make Maine safer: mental health investment, law enforcement accountability, and protecting the Second Amendment rights that define our way of life.

Matthew Hogan is a political commentator covering Maine politics.
