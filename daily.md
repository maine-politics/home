# Maine Roundup — August 19, 2026

### 🗳️ LePage widens lead in 2nd District as Dunlap struggles with turnout

Former Governor Paul LePage has opened a 7-point lead over Democrat Matt Dunlap in Maine's 2nd Congressional District race, according to a new poll from the University of New Hampshire Survey Center. LePage leads 48% to 41%, with 11% undecided. The poll shows LePage performing strongly among rural voters and men, while Dunlap's support is concentrated in Bangor and college towns. Dunlap's campaign is scrambling to boost turnout in Aroostook and Washington counties, where he underperformed in the primary. The LePage campaign, meanwhile, is airing a new ad tying Dunlap to national Democrats and President Miller's unpopular tariff policies. The Cook Political Report has shifted the race from "Likely Republican" to "Lean Republican."

### ⚖️ Collins campaign hits Jackson on logging stance as labor unions rally

Senator Susan Collins and her Democratic challenger Troy Jackson are clashing over the future of Maine's timber industry. Collins released a digital ad this week accusing Jackson, a logger by trade, of "selling out" Maine loggers by supporting environmental regulations that Collins says would "shutter sawmills and drive up home prices." Jackson fired back, calling the ad a "desperate lie," and pointed to his record fighting Canadian logging trucks as a state senator. Meanwhile, the Maine AFL-CIO endorsed Jackson on Tuesday, with union president Peter Curley calling him "the only candidate who's actually worked a day in the woods." The Maine logging industry supports roughly 15,000 jobs statewide, making it a central issue in both the Senate and 2nd District races. Women voters, who lean toward Collins in recent polling, are being courted aggressively by Jackson's campaign with a new bus tour of southern Maine.

### 🏛️ Lewiston memorial bill clears committee with bipartisan support

A bill to designate the site of the 2023 Lewiston shooting as a state memorial has unanimously passed the Joint Committee on Veterans and Legal Affairs. The bill, sponsored by Senator Peggy Rotundo (D-Lewiston), would allocate $500,000 for a permanent memorial and mental health resource center at the site. Families of the victims testified in support, including Ben Dyer, who lost his sister Tricia Asselin. "This isn't about politics," Dyer said. "It's about making sure we never forget what happened, and that we give people a place to heal." The bill now heads to the full Legislature for a vote next week. Republicans have signaled they will support the measure, though some have raised concerns about the cost amid budget negotiations.
